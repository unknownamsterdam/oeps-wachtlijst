## How it works

Does something break that should have lasted longer? Then you're entitled to a solution. That's called the legal warranty. For it, you need to show you bought it at that shop.

You can do that with the till receipt. But also with:

- your **bank statement** (date, amount and shop),
- the **order email** or invoice from a web shop,
- a **photo** of the receipt.

Bought it in a shop? Ask whether they can look up your purchase using the date, time and amount on your statement.

## Exchanging is something else

Something broken? Then it's about the warranty. Does it just not fit, or don't you like it? Then you want to exchange it. In a regular shop that isn't a right. The shop sets the rules, and almost always asks for the receipt.

## The tricky part

Receipts are small, disappear into coat pockets and fade in a drawer. And you only need them once they're gone. With big appliances, only after a year or two.

## What you can do

Take a photo of your receipt right after you buy something. That's all it takes. A photo or scan counts as proof, even when the paper later turns white.

Put the photo in OEPS! with the **Add a receipt or photo** button. It's saved there, with the date and the warranty. You don't need to link your email for this.

And the paper itself? For a warranty claim the photo is usually enough, so you can throw it away. Want to exchange something in a regular shop later? Then the shop may ask for the real receipt. Keep the paper a little longer in that case.
