## How it works

Your solar panels send power to the grid. Until now your supplier offset that power against what you take. That is net metering, or salderen.

From 1 January 2027 that scheme ends. You then get a **feed-in payment** (terugleververgoeding) for the power you send back. Your supplier may deduct **feed-in costs** (terugleverkosten) from it. What you keep depends on your contract.

According to Keuze.nl, for most suppliers that leaves very little. At Essent the feed-in payment is 4.74 cents per kWh on a one-year contract, with feed-in costs of 4.50 cents. That leaves 0.25 cents per kWh net. These figures come from the suppliers' own tariff announcements and can differ per contract.

## What to check

1. Which feed-in payment and which feed-in costs are in your new contract?
2. What is left over, per kWh? Subtract the costs from the payment.
3. Does your bill show the costs per kWh? From 2027 it must.
4. How long is your contract? Keuze.nl calculates that a one-year contract is cheaper for solar panel owners.

## The tricky part

Many people only notice when the yearly statement arrives. By then the money is gone, and the rule sits in a tariff sheet nobody reads. So check it once now, and let OEPS! flag every change.

Note: the rules are set for 2027, but changes are still being discussed. Check the ACM site or your supplier in the coming months.
