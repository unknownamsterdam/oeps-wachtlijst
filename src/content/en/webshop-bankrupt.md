## First: who is the curator?

When a webshop goes bankrupt, someone is appointed to wind up the company. This is called the **curator**. They keep track of who is owed what. This is the person you register with.

## Your order did not arrive and you paid in advance

Then you are a **creditor**. You register with the curator and show how much you paid.

1. Find the curator's name. It is often in the bankruptcy notice.
2. Send a short email: your name, your order number, the amount and the date.
3. Add your order confirmation and proof of payment.

You do not always get everything back. What is left depends on the company.

## Paid by credit card? Then there is an extra option

With a credit card you can ask for a **chargeback**. This is a refund through your credit card company. It works like this:

- First try to solve it with the shop. Keep your emails.
- If that does not work, ask your credit card company for the money back.
- This is for orders that were not delivered. Problems after delivery are not covered, for example a broken product.

The process can take 5 to 6 months. So start quickly.

## Warranty and broken products

- **The product breaks:** you have the right to a free repair or replacement. But your seller, the webshop, no longer exists.
- **Manufacturer's warranty:** report it to the manufacturer or importer.
- **Manufacturer also bankrupt?** Then the seller stays responsible. If that does not work, you can get part of your money back.

## The tricky part

The chance of getting money back from a bankrupt shop is small. So it matters to have proof **now**. Take a photo of your order confirmation and proof of payment, and keep your emails.

Please note: this is general information, not legal advice. If you are unsure, ask the curator or a legal aid helpline.
