## How it works

There are two kinds of warranty:

- **The manufacturer's or shop's warranty.** Often 1 or 2 years. They decide what's in it.
- **Your legal warranty.** You always have it. A product has to do what you can expect, for as long as you can expect.

Has the manufacturer's warranty ended? Your legal rights are still there.

## Per type of product

- **Washing machine, dryer, fridge, dishwasher.** Big appliances you use for years. For an expensive model the legal warranty can be 5 to 8 years, according to the ACM.
- **Laptop.** Here too the ACM mentions 5 to 8 years for an expensive laptop. A cheap laptop doesn't have to last as long.
- **Television.** A TV that breaks after 2 years without you doing anything wrong? Ask the shop for a solution.
- **Phone.** A €1,000 phone should last longer than a €150 one. Damage from drops or water isn't covered.
- **Small appliances, like an air fryer, hairdryer or kettle.** These also have to last a reasonable time. The more expensive, the more you can expect.

These aren't fixed numbers. It always depends on the price, the brand, what was promised and how you use it. Electronics shops often work with a table of how long products last. Ask about it.

## The tricky part

After 2 years almost everyone thinks it's over. You don't even look for your receipt any more. And that's exactly when you need it. See [warranty over, now what?](/en/warranty/expired/) for an example email to the shop.
