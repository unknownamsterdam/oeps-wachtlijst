## What changes?

The European Batteries Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/1542) says devices with a **portable battery** must be built so you can **remove and replace** the battery yourself. That is Article 11. The rule applies from **18 February 2027**.

It covers batteries up to 5 kilos. That includes phones and tablets, but also many other devices.

## How easy does it have to be?

- With **commonly available tools**.
- If a special tool is needed, it must be **supplied free**.
- It must not be necessary to use heat or solvents.
- The new battery must still let the device work well and safely.
- The manufacturer must sell the battery as a spare part for at least **five years**, at a reasonable price.

## What is not certain yet?

There are **exceptions**. The article of the law names, among others, devices for wet environments and certain medical devices. A new European rule also brings exceptions for smart watches and other wearables.

On **phones** the stories differ. Androidplanet writes that phones with IP67 water resistance, or a battery that keeps 80 percent after 1,000 charge cycles, are exempt. The legal summaries we read don't say that. Smartphones and tablets also have their own European rules (Regulation (EU) 2023/1670). So we don't yet know how many phones will get an exemption.

## Does it apply to my phone?

Probably not to a phone you already have. The rule is about products placed on the market. We can't be sure.

## What about your warranty?

The EU text we read doesn't cover it. Whether replacing a battery yourself affects your warranty is decided by the manufacturer's terms. Read those first.

Broken after the warranty? Also read [right to repair](/en/explained/right-to-repair/) and [how long a warranty usually lasts](/en/explained/how-long-warranty/).
