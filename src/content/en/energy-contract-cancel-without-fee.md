## What has changed?

Since **1 January 2026** the new **Energy Act** (Energiewet) applies. Because of this, suppliers adjust their terms. They must inform you **at least 30 days in advance**.

Some suppliers let customers with a fixed contract switch without a fee because of this. In spring 2026, for example, Vandebron and Essent did. Whether your supplier does, you read in its message.

## Can you always leave without a fee?

No. You may only cancel without a fee if your supplier chooses to make a change itself, or if the change is to your disadvantage. A change imposed by the government does not automatically give that right.

## When do you cancel?

1. **Wait for your supplier's message.** If you cancel earlier, you may still get a cancellation fee.
2. **Check the deadline.** If you do nothing, you accept the new terms.
3. **Check whether you get a welcome discount.** You often lose it if you leave early.

## Why it can cost you money

- **You lose your welcome discount.** It can be more than €400.
- **The cheap period is short.** Most fixed contracts run for a year. Near the end of winter, switching saves little.
- **A large part of your bill you cannot influence.** Network costs and taxes stay the same.

Radar writes that, according to experts, switching costs money in most cases. Keuze.nl calculates an example where switching does pay off. So always calculate it for your own situation.

## Variable contract

Do you have a variable contract? Then you can always switch. You may consider fixing your rate.
